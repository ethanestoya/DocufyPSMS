Step 3 of 4 · Add-ons

Add-ons as a dropdown,
not a wall of cards.

A UI proposal for the customer print request. One add-ons field opens the shop's supplies list; whatever the customer picks drops into a short editable list, and the totals feed Step 4 exactly as they do today.

The ask. “Make the Step 3 add-ons of the customer print request a dropdown style instead.” — reviewer, November 2026
What changes

The card-per-add-on grid is replaced by a single Add-ons field. Opening it lists the supplies the shop actually stocks, with price, unit and stock. Chosen items move into a compact list where quantities are set. The step stops growing as the catalog grows.

What does not change

Add-ons stay optional. The price rule (unit price × quantity), the selectedAddons payload, the Step 4 breakdown and the admin-managed inventory list are untouched. The step still sits between Print Options and Review & Complete.

Every add-on is always on screen

The step renders one full card per add-on in the inventory's Add-ons category, each carrying its own quantity controls, even when the customer wants none of them.

  • 1Every add-on is always expanded. The step is as long as the shop's catalog, so a customer looking for one item reads every card first.
  • 2Quantity controls appear at zero. A card with a − 0 + row reads as something to configure, not something to add — and it repeats on every card.
  • 3The “supplies” chip is internal. It is the inventory category, not a distinction a customer chooses by.
  • 4Stock is known but not shown. The store already flags inStock, yet an out-of-stock add-on looks exactly like a selectable one.
  • 5Two boxes say the same thing. “Need additional supplies?” repeats the step title, and the total only appears after something is already chosen.
  • 6Optional is never stated. Nothing on the screen tells the customer they can move on without picking anything.

Behaviour, copy and edge cases
SPEC

How it would actually work

Everything below happens on the customer print request only. The staff walk-in flow shares the same component, so it would inherit the change unless we gate it.

StateWhat the customer sees and what happens
Nothing selected The field reads Select add-ons with no count chip. Under it, one quiet line: “No add-ons selected — you can continue without any.” The step never blocks Next.
List open The field takes a blue border and halo. The panel opens directly under it, listing only active Add-ons items from the inventory with unit price and live stock. Picking a row adds it with quantity 1 and keeps the panel open, so several can be picked in one go.
Selecting / unselecting A tap anywhere on the row toggles it. Selected rows are tinted and carry a check. The panel footer and the field chip update the moment a row changes.
Closing Done, Esc, or a click/tap outside the panel. Focus returns to the field. The chosen items stay in the list below.
Quantity −/+ in steps of 1; line and card totals update live, and going below 1 removes the item. Quantity stays typeable as it is today — someone ordering 25 pens should not tap 24 times.
Out of stock Row stays visible but disabled, with “Out of stock” in red — hiding it makes a supply the shop normally carries look discontinued. An item already selected when stock runs out keeps its row with a red note and can be removed; the order is never silently changed.
No add-ons at all The existing empty state is kept (“No add-ons are currently available.”), and the field is not rendered.
Many add-ons The list scrolls inside the panel (max ~5 rows) instead of lengthening the page. Past roughly 8 items, a small search box goes at the top of the panel — not before.
Keyboard / screen reader The field is a button with aria-expanded, labelled “Add-ons, 1 selected”. The panel is a labelled group of checkboxes, so Space toggles and arrow keys read naturally; nothing depends on hover.
Totals Unchanged: unit price × quantity, summed into the card total and into Step 4's “Add-ons” line. No new money rule.

Choices for the reviewer
DECISIONS

Four things to confirm

The mockups use the recommendation in each row. Say the word if any should go the other way before this is built.

1 · How the dropdown is shaped
Recommended: A

A · One picker for the whole step. A single field lists every add-on and builds a selected list below it. Short, consistent, and it does not grow with the catalog.

B · One dropdown per add-on. Every add-on keeps a row and only the quantity is a dropdown. Closer to “dropdown” in the narrowest sense, but the step still grows with the catalog and the range hides inside a menu.

Variant B, for comparison:
Ballpen (Black) · ₱10 / piece 1
Staples · ₱5 / box 0
2 · Quantity editing

Recommend: −/+ stepper on each selected row, and the number stays typeable. Stepper is what phones need; typing is what a 25-piece order needs.

Alternative: dropdown of 1–10. Rejected because it caps the order and adds a tap for every change.

3 · Out-of-stock add-ons

Recommend: show them, disabled, with “Out of stock”. The inventory already knows; the customer should too, rather than wonder where a supply went.

Alternative: hide them until restocked — fewer rows, but the catalog looks like it changes at random.

4 · Where the total lives

Recommend: both ends — a running chip on the field (2 selected · ₱15.00) and the total in the selected list's header. Step 4 keeps its own Add-ons line, unchanged. The step should never make the customer open it to see what it has cost them.

Also worth deciding
  • •Does this apply to the staff walk-in flow too? It shares the component. Recommendation: yes — one add-ons experience in both flows.
  • •“Next Step” label. Recommendation: keep it, and lean on the helper line for “optional” rather than renaming the button for one step.
  • •Search inside the panel. Recommendation: only once the shop actually stocks more than ~8 add-ons; adding it now would be a field that is usually empty.
  • •The internal “supplies” chip. Recommendation: drop it from the customer side; the category already decides what appears here.

The change stays inside the step-3 block of src/app/components/shared/PrintTransaction.tsx, reusing the existing selectedAddons state and totals. No store, pricing or order changes are needed.
